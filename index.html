A FIELD GUIDE TO BRAIN HEALTH

Your brain isn't fixed.
It's tended.

Five daily practices shape how well you sleep, think, and feel, backed by real evidence, not another wellness trend. This guide lays out what actually matters, and what to leave alone.

Start reading ↓ Take the check-in

Brain health isn't one thing you fix. It's five ordinary things you keep doing: sleeping enough, eating in a pattern that feeds your neurons, moving your body, managing stress, and using your mind in ways that actually build it. None of them are exotic. None of them need a supplement. This page walks through each one, with the practices that have real evidence behind them and the confidence level for each claim, so you can tell a proven habit from a promising guess.

01 · REST

Sleep

Deep sleep is when your brain clears the metabolic waste it built up during the day, files memories into longer-term storage, and resets the prefrontal circuits you need for focus and mood the next day. Cut it short often enough and all three of those jobs go undone.

  • Keep the same wake-up time every day, weekends included. Regularity does more for your circadian clock than any single "ideal" bedtime.

    Strong

  • Most adults function best on 7 or more hours. Use how you feel through the day as your real gauge, not a number you read somewhere.

    Strong

  • Get outside for bright light within an hour of waking. It's one of the strongest signals your circadian clock responds to.

    Moderate

  • Wind down stimulating screens and bright light in the hour before bed. The content and timing matter more than the blue light itself.

    Mixed

  • Keep the bedroom cool, dark, and quiet. Small environmental changes reliably shorten how long it takes you to fall asleep.

    Moderate

02 · FUEL

Nutrition

The brain runs on a steady supply of glucose and depends on fats to build and repair neuron membranes. No single food changes that. What consistently shows up in the research is the overall pattern of what you eat, not any one ingredient.

  • Build meals around vegetables, fruit, whole grains, legumes, and fish or other omega-3 sources. This Mediterranean-style pattern has the strongest evidence of any diet studied for brain health.

    Strong

  • Stay hydrated through the day. Even mild dehydration measurably drags down mood and concentration.

    Moderate

  • Limit ultra-processed food and added sugar, less because any single food is dangerous, more because it crowds out the foods above.

    Moderate

  • Fermented foods support gut health, and the gut-brain link is a real, active area of research, though it's still early days for specific claims.

    Mixed

  • Morning caffeine can sharpen focus. Afternoon or evening caffeine quietly undermines the sleep you're trying to protect.

    Strong

03 · CIRCULATION

Movement

Physical activity increases blood flow to the brain and raises levels of a protein called BDNF, which supports new connections between neurons. Of everything on this page, movement has some of the most consistent evidence behind it, at every age.

  • Aim for roughly 150 minutes of moderate activity a week, a brisk walk counts, per WHO guidance, plus movement on most days if you can manage it.

    Strong

  • Add strength training twice a week. It isn't only about muscle, it's linked to better cognitive performance too.

    Strong

  • Short movement breaks during long stretches of sitting beat one long workout for how alert you feel that day.

    Moderate

  • New, complex movement, dance, an unfamiliar sport, challenges the brain more than repeating the same routine.

    Moderate

  • Moving outdoors adds a mood benefit that the same movement indoors doesn't fully match.

    Moderate

04 · RECOVERY

Calm

A short burst of stress sharpens focus. Stress that never lets up raises cortisol, which over time can shrink the hippocampus, the part of the brain most involved in memory, and weaken the prefrontal circuits behind focus and self-control.

  • Real, in-person connection is one of the strongest buffers against stress and later cognitive decline, and the thing people tend to drop first when busy.

    Strong

  • A regular breathing or meditation practice measurably lowers your baseline stress response within a matter of weeks.

    Strong

  • Time outdoors lowers rumination and stress markers, even a short walk does something measurable.

    Moderate

  • Naming what you're feeling, out loud or on paper, reliably takes some of the intensity out of it.

    Moderate

  • You can't eliminate stress, but building in real recovery afterward matters more than trying to avoid it altogether.

    Moderate

05 · SHARPNESS

Mind

Attention and memory are genuinely trainable, but the training that works looks less like a brain-training app and more like real engagement, spaced practice, and protecting the sleep and movement that make clear thinking possible in the first place.

  • Single-tasking beats multitasking for both speed and accuracy. Constant switching between tasks carries a real, measurable cost.

    Strong

  • Spaced repetition, learning something once and revisiting it days later, beats cramming for what you actually retain.

    Strong

  • Commercial brain-training games make you better at that specific game. Evidence that it transfers to broader intelligence is weak.

    Mixed

  • A deliberate break, a short walk, staring out a window, helps consolidate what you just learned more than pushing straight through.

    Moderate

  • Learning a genuinely new skill, an instrument, a language, keeps building new neural connections throughout life, more than passive input like scrolling.

    Moderate

WORTH RETIRING

Four things you can stop believing

"You only use 10% of your brain."

False

Brain imaging shows activity spread across nearly the whole brain over a day. Different regions take turns, but very little sits permanently idle.

"Brain-training apps make you generally smarter."

Mixed at best

You reliably get better at the specific game. Evidence that it transfers to broader memory or intelligence is weak and inconsistent.

"One bad night of sleep undoes your progress."

False

It's the pattern across weeks that shapes brain health, not any single night. One rough night is normal, not a setback to panic over.

"A brain supplement will sharpen your mind."

Consistently disappointing

For people who are already well-nourished, most studied supplements show no measurable cognitive benefit beyond correcting an actual deficiency.

One honest note. Everything on this page is general education, not a diagnosis or a treatment plan. If sleep, mood, memory, or focus feel persistently off, not just an off week, that's worth bringing to a doctor or a licensed therapist. They can see things a website never can.
